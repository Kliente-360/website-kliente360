**Every free add-on** on an enterprise platform carries the same implicit question: free up to where? Salesforce Foundations is the latest version of that test. Launched in September 2024 as a zero-cost SKU for customers already paying for Enterprise Edition of Sales Cloud or Service Cloud, the package picked up real weight through 2025 when it started including Agentforce — turning into, for a lot of companies that already had the base contract, the cheapest entry point to test an AI agent in production without signing a new license.

The problem is that "free" and "enough" rarely line up on an enterprise platform, and Foundations is no exception. The package handles a proof of concept or a small use case with room to spare. Deciding whether it handles the company's actual operation means looking at the four limits the marketing page doesn't print in the same font size as the "$0."

## What's included for free — and why it isn't a small amount

Foundations isn't a disguised trial. It's a set of real capabilities, unlocked permanently, with no extra contract, for whoever is already on the right edition:

1. **Sales.** Full Sales Console, lead and opportunity management, quoting tools and secure Pay Now payment links straight from a quote or opportunity.
2. **Service.** Service console, case management, macros for repetitive-task automation and multi-channel support.
3. **Marketing.** Drag-and-drop email builder, built-in campaign analytics and up to 2,000 email sends a month.
4. **Commerce.** One direct-to-consumer digital storefront, managed checkout, merchandising tools and the same secure payment links from the Sales module.
5. **Data 360.** Unified customer profile across systems, segmentation across up to five data streams and 10,000 annual segmentation and activation credits.
6. **Agentforce.** Agent Builder, Prompt Builder, at least one configured agent skill and a credit pool (Flex Credits) shared with Data 360 to test a real use case, not just a demo.

> Foundations isn't a sales hook — it's real production capability, just with the usage meter already running from day one.

That's already more than most enterprise CRM competitors give away for free. But "real capability" isn't the same as "sufficient capability" — and that's where the four limits come in.

## Where the free tier stops: the four limits that decide if it's enough

None of the limits below are hidden — they all show up in fine print in the official documentation. What changes is how much each one weighs depending on the company's size and use case.

1. **The minimum edition requires Enterprise.** Foundations only exists for customers already paying Enterprise Edition or higher of Sales Cloud and/or Service Cloud. Customers on Starter Suite or Professional Edition — the segment that would benefit most from a free boost — don't get access. Government Cloud and Industry Clouds customers are also excluded, regardless of edition.
2. **Marketing caps at 2,000 emails a month.** Enough for a small base newsletter, not enough for any email operation running real nurture cadences. Going past the cap means a paid upgrade, not a quiet overage.
3. **AI credit and data credit share the same pool.** Agentforce and Data 360 draw from the same Flex Credits balance. Whoever runs heavy segmentation in Data 360 leaves less credit to run an agent — and vice versa. That reproduces, at a smaller scale, [the same operation multiplier that already decides the budget for paid Data Cloud](/blog/en/data-cloud-pricing-creditos-2026.html): the real cost isn't in the entry price, it's in which function burns through credit fastest.
4. **Commerce doesn't reach every country — Brazil included.** The direct-to-consumer storefront in the Commerce module is officially available only in the United States, according to research from Salesforce Ben. A Brazilian company that saw free Commerce as a reason to activate Foundations finds out, right when configuring it, that this specific module doesn't exist for them.

That fourth point is the one that most surprises Brazilian managers. It isn't a usage limit — it's an absence of regional availability, and it only shows up after the company has already decided, on paper, that Foundations was worth activating for the full bundle.

## Why the credit number changes depending on which source you check

Worth flagging a symptom, not just a limit: searching "how many Flex Credits does Foundations include" returns different answers depending on the date of the source — 100,000, 200,000, 450,000. It isn't a widespread typo. It's Salesforce revising the Agentforce credit ruler across multiple rounds since launch, the same dynamic that [already reshaped Data Cloud pricing into unified credits plus a per-profile SKU in March 2026](/blog/en/data-cloud-pricing-creditos-2026.html) and that ran alongside [the rebranding that turned Sales Cloud into Agentforce Sales](/blog/en/sales-cloud-vira-agentforce-sales.html) in the same cycle.

For whoever owns the budget decision, the practical lesson is simple: don't lock the business case to a credit number read on a third-party blog published six months ago. Confirm the balance currently active in your own org before projecting how long the pilot will run before needing an additional purchase.

## Three questions to decide if Foundations solves the problem or just delays the real decision

Foundations is a cheap testing tool, not a permanent operating base. Three questions, in the order worth checking before treating it as long-term strategy:

1. **Does the use case fit inside the limits, or is it already bigger than them?** If the real volume of email, segmentation or agent conversation blows past the cap in the first month, Foundations isn't the right base — it's just the pilot that confirms the paid investment is necessary, and the choice between [fixed $2 conversations and paid Flex Credits](/blog/en/agentforce-pricing-seis-modelos.html) starts deciding the budget from there.
2. **Is the company already on Enterprise Edition, or would it need to upgrade edition just to qualify?** Upgrading edition to access a free add-on is a real cost decision, not a zero-cost one — [the same ROI ruler that decides any Salesforce investment](/blog/en/salesforce-roi-matriz.html) applies here just the same.
3. **Is the module you care about available in the company's region?** Before designing the pilot around Commerce, confirm regional availability. Finding out about the restriction after committing the quarter's roadmap costs more than the license the company was trying to avoid paying for.

None of these three questions invalidate Foundations — they just avoid the mistake of treating it as a permanent substitute for an investment the actual operation is going to require anyway.

## The free tier proves the use case — it doesn't replace the investment decision

Foundations does well the job Salesforce designed it for: giving a customer who already pays for Enterprise Edition a way to test Agentforce, Data 360 and email automation without signing a new contract. That has real value, especially for a company still deciding whether an AI agent solves a concrete problem or is just a market trend.

The mistake is reading "free" as "solved." The four limits — minimum edition, email cap, shared credit pool and regional availability — aren't accidental fine print. They're the deliberate design of an acquisition product: show value fast, enough to justify the sales conversation about what comes next. A company that treats Foundations as phase one of a plan, not as a final destination, gets the right value out of it.

## Questions that keep coming back

To close, the most common questions about what Salesforce Foundations really covers.

## What is Salesforce Foundations?

Salesforce Foundations is a free add-on (a $0 SKU) that Salesforce grants to Sales Cloud and/or Service Cloud customers on Enterprise Edition or higher, with no extra license fee. It bundles basic sales, service, marketing (email), Commerce, data unification via Data 360, and an initial pool of Agentforce credits to test an AI agent in production. Launched in September 2024, it picked up Agentforce as a module months after the original announcement.

## Who can use Salesforce Foundations for free?

Only customers already paying for Enterprise Edition or higher of Sales Cloud and/or Service Cloud. Customers on Starter Suite, Essentials or Professional Edition don't qualify, even while paying for Salesforce — Agentforce in particular requires Enterprise Edition as a technical floor, which leaves small companies out of both the paid and the free version. Government Cloud and Industry Clouds customers are also excluded from eligibility, regardless of edition.

## Does Salesforce Foundations work for a Brazilian company?

It does, with one concrete caveat: the Commerce module — a direct-to-consumer digital storefront with integrated checkout and payment — is officially available only in the United States, which excludes Brazilian companies from that specific module. The other modules (Sales, Service, Marketing, Data 360 and Agentforce) don't carry that regional restriction and work normally for anyone already on the eligible edition in Brazil.
